### Abstract

We investigate the continuous function \(f\) defined by 

\[x\mapsto \sum_{\sigma\le_L x}2^{-K(\sigma)}\]

as a variant of Chaitin's Omega from the perspective of analysis, computability, and algorithmic randomness. Among other results, we obtain that: 
- \(f\) is differentiable precisely at density random points; 
- \(f(x)\) is \(x\)-random if and only if \(x\) is weakly low for \(K\) (low for \(\Omega\)); 
- the range of \(f\) is a null, nowhere dense, perfect \(\Pi^0_1(\emptyset')\) class with Hausdorff dimension \(1\); 
- \(f(x)\oplus x\ge_T\emptyset'\) for all \(x\); 
- there are \(2^{\aleph_0}\) many \(x\) such that \(f(x)\) is not 1-random; 
- \(f\) is not Turing invariant but is Turing invariant on the ideal of \(K\)-trivial reals. 

We also discuss the connection between \(f\) and other variants of Omega.
