### Abstract

Chaitin's Omega is defined to be the halting probability of a randomly constructed program. Equivalently it can be defined by the sum of Kolmogorov weights of all binary strings. In this talk we investigate the Omega function \(f\) that maps a real \(x\) to the sum of Kolmogorov weights of all strings on the left of \(x\). We establish properties of \(f\) and compare it with previously defined Omega-related functions. We present that
- \(f\) is strictly increasing, continuous and differentiable precisely at density random reals; 
- the range of \(f\) is a null, nowhere dense, perfect \(\Pi^0_1(0')\) class with Hausdorff dimension \(1\); 
- there are continuum many \(x\) such that \(f(x)\) is not random.
